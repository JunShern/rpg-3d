[Act Two. A riverbank made of two flats and a blue cloth. PERFORMANCE begins. Audience mood: STONE. No role skill has moved it since the curtain rose.]

MESSENGER: My lord, the town has hung a sheet from the mill. They ask your terms.
TYRANT: Terms! Does the wolf give terms to the fold?

[A cough. Three hundred men not moving.]

TYRANT: They have had my terms since spring. A rope, a ditch, and a short prayer.
PASK: (under his breath) Ines. They're not booing. Why aren't they booing?
INES: (from the prompt corner) Keep going. 'Bring up the line.'
TYRANT: Bring up the line! Let every musket speak, and let the river carry word of it to the sea!

[Six wooden muskets come up to the shoulder.]

TYRANT: Fire.

[Nobody on stage has moved yet when a chair scrapes. A sergeant-major in the front row is on her feet with her hat in her hand. She is not shouting.]

CALDER: No.
CALDER: Beg pardon. No. He didn't laugh. Nobody laughed.
PASK: Madam, this is a theatre.
CALDER: It's my drill hall, sir. I signed the chit.
HALE: (from the wings) Mistress Varrow. Bring the curtain in. Now, please.

[PERFORMANCE: three commands are lit.]

> STRIKE. Hale silences the house.
  HALE: By the authority of the Revels Office, this performance is suspended and the hall will...
  CALDER: Sit down, son. I've been dismissed by better.
  [The house laughs once, like a dog barking. Strike fails. Mood: STONE.]
> PLAY THROUGH. Pask drowns her out.
  TYRANT: Let every musket SPEAK, and let the river...
  CALDER: They don't speak. You've never heard one, have you. It isn't speaking.
  [Pask dries. It is the first time in forty years. Mood: STONE.]
> GIVE THE STAGE.
  [Ines closes the book on her finger.]

[Ines walks out of the prompt corner into the light. She takes the prop letter from Fenn and holds it out over the footlights.]

INES: Then tell it. The Messenger has the next line.
CALDER: I'm no actor.
INES: No. Up you come.

[Calder climbs onto the stage. GUEST ROLE: Calder, as the Messenger. Mood: LISTENING.]

CALDER: There wasn't a messenger. Order came down the line by drum and flag, same as any order.
CALDER: I was second file. I could see the sheet on the mill. We all could.
CALDER: Captain sent back up the line. 'Town shows white. Confirm.'
CALDER: And we stood there. You could hear the mill wheel.
CALDER: Then it came again. Same as before. Three words.

[Ines's left hand is moving against the cover of the prompt book.]

CALDER: 'Clear the ford.'
INES: (not quite aloud) Clear the ford.

[They say it together. Only Fenn, a yard away, hears.]

CALDER: That's all. Nobody said wolves. Nobody said anything. Sergeant said 'Present,' because that is what comes next.
SOLDIER: Sit down, Ruth.
CALDER: I've sat down ten years, Tam.

[She turns to Pask and gives a small, correct nod.]

CALDER: You can have your crown back, sir. I only wanted it said right the once.

[She hands the letter to Ines, and looks at her a moment longer than she needs to.]

CALDER: You've a steady hand, miss. For theatre people.

[She steps down. Nobody claps. The six actors with wooden muskets, uncued, ground them. In the front row a dozen men do the same, by reflex, with nothing in their hands.]

[PERFORMANCE WON: As It Happened. The Messenger learns PLAIN REPORT. Greywether will remember this.]

PASK: (whispering) What do I do? Ines. What's my line?
INES: There isn't one. Curtain.
FENN: Ines, you said it. You said it when she...
INES: Curtain, Fenn.
