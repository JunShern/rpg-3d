[Backstage in the drill hall. A musket rack has been pushed aside to make a dressing room. Through the curtain: three hundred soldiers finding their seats.]

FENN: They're all sitting in rows. Nobody told them to. They just did it.
INES: They're soldiers. It's that or a square.

[The player has Ines. Walk the wings: the props table, the curtain, the costume crate.]

[At the props table.]

INES: Crown. Letter. Six muskets, wooden. Blood bladder.
INES: Who moved the letter?
PASK: I did. A king does not go looking for his correspondence.
INES: A king gets it from the Messenger in Act Two, off this table, where it lives.
PASK: Tyranny.
INES: Yes. Yours. Eight minutes.

[At the curtain. Hale has one eye to the gap and is counting under his breath.]

HALE: Three hundred and eleven. Nine officers. I would like it noted that I advised against this town.
INES: Noted.
HALE: And the volley scene?

> It's cut. As you asked.
  HALE: Good. Thank you.
  HALE: I don't enjoy it, you know. It is rather a good scene.
> The Fool describes it. Nobody fires.
  HALE: That is not a cut. That is a paraphrase.
  HALE: Is it in couplets? No. Don't tell me. If you tell me I shall have to write it down.
> You'll see it when they do.
  HALE: Mistress Varrow. I have a pencil and a very small amount of courage.
  HALE: Please do not make me use both in one evening.

[At the costume crate. Clew is sewing a button onto the Widow's glove. She does not look up.]

CLEW: Your hands.
INES: They're fine.
CLEW: They're doing a drum call. On your skirt. The left one.

[Ines stops.]

INES: Habit. I'm counting the house in.
CLEW: Mm. Hold this. No, taut.
CLEW: I've dressed this play thirty times. Do you know the only house that ever frightened me?
INES: Soldiers.
CLEW: Children. A schools matinee. They believe every word, and then they go home and tell it.
CLEW: This lot were there. You can't tell them a thing. So don't try. Say the lines as they're set down and let them sit in it.

> As they're set down?
  CLEW: Word for word. It was written by somebody who knew.
  INES: Knew what? She wasn't at the Ford. Nobody who was at the Ford wrote plays afterwards.
  CLEW: She knew what it was for.
> And if the lines are wrong?
  CLEW: Wrong.
  INES: If one of them stands up and says it wasn't like that.
  CLEW: Then he's a critic, and we've had those before.

[Clew bites the thread.]

CLEW: There. Don't let Aurelio pull it off in the faint. He acts with his teeth.

[Fenn, back from the curtain.]

FENN: Ines. There's a woman in the front row with her hat on her knee. Sergeant's stripes.
FENN: She hasn't moved. She's looking at the curtain like it owes her money.
INES: That's who booked us.
FENN: Why would she book us? Is that good? It doesn't feel good.
INES: Go and stand by for the Fool's entrance.
FENN: That's not for twenty minutes.
INES: Then you'll be early.

[Ines alone in the prompt corner. The house lamps go down. Her left hand starts the drum call again. She puts the prompt book on top of it.]

INES: Places.
INES: Stand by, curtain.

[Hold the button to raise the curtain. It does not rise until the player does it.]

INES: Go.
