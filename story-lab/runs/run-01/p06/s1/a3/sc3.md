[The tannery yard. Thunder lies along the south wall with his chin on a hay bale. Sol is mashing turnips. The player is partway through the feed: three presses to fill the bowl, and this interrupts at two.]

DESSEN: Keeper. Visitor. Be nice.
SOL: I'm always nice.
DESSEN: You told the mayor his children were too loud for the phoenix.
SOL: They were.

[An old man at the gate on an orderly's arm, in a dress uniform forty years out of date. A great many medals. He stops and looks.]

GLASS: Is that the one?
DESSEN: That's him, Marshal.
GLASS: He's smaller.
SOL: He's lying down.
GLASS: Who's this?
DESSEN: The keeper from Skarn Fell, sir. She has charge of him.
GLASS: Charge! I had charge of him eleven years and I never once had charge of him.
GLASS: Good. Good. Girl. Does he still take the left turn wide?
SOL: He doesn't fly, sir. The wing went at the Karst.
GLASS: The Karst. Yes. We won the Karst, you know.

[Glass lets go of the orderly and walks up to Thunder, on the blind side.]

> 'Other side, sir. He can't see you there.'
  GLASS: Can't see...
  [He goes round the long way, slowly, one hand on the wall.]
> Say nothing. Let them find each other.
  [Thunder startles. A crack of static jumps to the gatepost. The orderly swears. Glass does not flinch.]
  GLASS: There he is.

GLASS: Hello, old storm.

[Thunder's good eye moves over him. It moves on to the turnips.]

GLASS: He doesn't know me.
SOL: He doesn't see much. He goes by voice, mostly. And smell.
GLASS: I have the same voice.

> 'It's the brass, sir. He's never liked a uniform.'
  GLASS: Ha! No. He ate a bugle once. Whole. Did I say?
  [It is not true about the brass. Dessen looks at Sol and says nothing.]
> 'It's been a long time, sir.'
  GLASS: Thirty-eight years.
  GLASS: Yes. Well. It has.

GLASS: I used to do a thing, before a call. Hand flat, here, under the jaw. He'd lean on it.

[He does not do it. He looks at the bowl instead.]

GLASS: What is that you give him?
SOL: Turnip. Mashed. He's lost the back teeth.
GLASS: So have I. Give it here.

[Glass takes the bowl. The feed prompt leaves the screen. The player watches him do the third one, badly. Thunder eats.]

GLASS: The captain tells me there are three left in him.
SOL: Three.
GLASS: I had forty. They bound him to me with forty and I thought it was all the calls in the world.
GLASS: I spent six on parades.
GLASS: A young man came last month with a paper. They bring me a good many papers. Was that this?
DESSEN: It was the recall, sir. It needed a summoner's seal.
GLASS: Ah.
GLASS: Well. He'd want to go. He always wanted to go. You couldn't hold him.
SOL: He wants turnips and a south wall, sir.
GLASS: That's what you see, girl. You've only known him old.

[Somewhere over the wall a drum starts. Thunder's head comes up off the bale and turns south.]

GLASS: There. You see?
SOL: That's the drum. He does that for weather. He does it for the dinner gong.

[Glass puts his hand flat under the jaw at last. Thunder allows it, the way he allows the farrier.]

GLASS: Good lad. Good lad.
GLASS: I should like to go home now.

[The orderly takes his arm. At the gate he turns.]

GLASS: Keeper. When it's time. Put your hand there. Flat. He likes it.
SOL: Yes, sir.

[He goes. Sol picks up the bowl. Thunder is still facing south.]

DESSEN: Which is it? The gong or the guns?
SOL: I don't know.
SOL: Don't tell anyone I said that.
