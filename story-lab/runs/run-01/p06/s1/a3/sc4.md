[The tannery yard, early. Brine is in the trough. Dessen comes in with an order she has folded very small. The ledger by the gate reads: THUNDER 3. BRINE 5. ASH 1.]

DESSEN: Staff want Skybreak. Noon tomorrow, over the river, where both armies can see it.
SOL: At what?
DESSEN: At nothing. At the sky. It's a demonstration.
SOL: A call.
DESSEN: One call.
SOL: Of three.
DESSEN: I can count, Keeper.

[She sits down on the feed bin, which she has never done before.]

DESSEN: Listen. That wall is held by bakers and boys. Two regiments went home last week, and I use the phrase loosely.
DESSEN: The general's reasoning is that if the town sees Old Thunder break the sky, it holds another month.
DESSEN: And if the other side sees it, they may not come at all. He could save two by spending one.
SOL: Do you think that?
DESSEN: I think it isn't stupid. I've seen a town hold on less. I watched Varsk hold for nine days on a rumour.
SOL: And if they come anyway?
DESSEN: Then we have two.

[Brine blows a bubble.]

SOL: What happens if I say no?
DESSEN: You can't say no. You're a civilian attached to remounts.
DESSEN: I can say no, for about a day. Then they send somebody who doesn't know he needs his turnips mashed.
DESSEN: So I'm asking what you would have me say.

> 'Do it. One, to save two.'
  SOL: Tell them yes. Noon. I stand with him and nobody else does.
  DESSEN: Agreed.
  SOL: And it isn't a parade. No band.
  DESSEN: There will absolutely be a band.
  [LEDGER: Thunder 3, marked for noon. The town's morale will rise. The number changes tomorrow, in front of everyone.]
> 'No. Not for a show.'
  SOL: Tell them he's lame. Tell them he's off his feed. Tell them I'm difficult.
  DESSEN: That last they'll believe.
  DESSEN: It buys a day. And it is my name on the refusal. I want you to know that I know that.
  [LEDGER unchanged. Dessen has taken a risk for you. The wall will be colder tonight.]
> 'They want the sky to do something. Does it have to be him?'
  DESSEN: Go on.
  SOL: Brine has five. He can't flood a valley now. He can still stand a river on its end for a minute.
  SOL: From the far bank they won't know what did it.
  DESSEN: You'd spend the horse.
  SOL: I'd spend one of five before one of three. Don't look at me like that. I can count as well.
  [Brine looks up at his name.]
  SOL: Sorry, old lad.
  [LEDGER: Brine 5, marked for noon. The staff will be half satisfied, which is its own problem.]

[Dessen stands and unfolds the order, and folds it again the other way.]

DESSEN: I'll take it up.
DESSEN: For what it's worth. When they had Garnet, nobody asked me anything. They used her until there wasn't any.
SOL: Is that why you ask?
DESSEN: It's why I count.

[She goes. The player has Sol again. The ledger can be examined. The numbers on it do not change until noon tomorrow.]
